# Ramayana inspiration and the Lantern journey

The Lantern story follows a courier who becomes responsible for a captive community. Its rescue mission, difficult crossings, animal allies and victory through cooperation draw on the Ramayana. Aether, Mira, the Regent, the three rune stones and the cannon dragon are original fiction. They are not representations of Rama, Sita, Ravana or a canonical battle. The illustrated sanctuary palette, natural stone, ivory armor and amber light remain the visual foundation.

## Characters and source traditions

These notes use specific episodes from the Sanskrit Valmiki Ramayana in the linked translations. Other Ramayana traditions differ. A narrative obstacle is not automatically a permanent biological weakness; fuel meters, attack cooldowns, damage windows and equipment progression below are game inventions.

## The playable story

The opening has three six-second beats: the Regent steals the dawn; Aether promises to cross the divided worlds; Mira asks him to restore wind, water and flame and bring the captives home. It lasts eighteen seconds when allowed to finish, with skip handled by the interface. Its art depicts the promise, the crossing and the confrontation.

The journey progresses from **last courier**, to **bearer of embers**, to **wayfinder of the lost**, to **knight of the lantern**. These titles follow the collected stones; they do not silently alter weapon statistics. Mira supplies the personal motive. Orren reveals the engine's failure and the guardian's recovery window. The three restored crossings make the community's rescue an action the player performs before the final confrontation.

The testing armory may expose every power immediately. That mode must be visibly identified as a test configuration; journey titles still communicate narrative progress independently of unlocked equipment.

## Portal crossings and action puzzles

Every selectable world keeps its original main route and gains three portal trials at the final heart foyer. Each starts with three elevated switch puzzles and guarded courts, then opens into a 104-landing action route with fourteen rescue relays and four named sections. A bound sentinel guards the final oath; defeating it opens the guardian seal, and the oath switch restores one of the three heart seals. The route uses no forced ten-minute timer or arbitrary locked waiting room.

| Crossing | Physical identity | Player action | Recovery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Windward Canopy | Upward and lateral airflow around high stone bells, followed by climbs reaching 660 units above the entry. The four sections vary height and crosswind strength. | Reach the bell levers, then use aerial currents across the high relay circuit while avoiding flying defenders. | Resting courts refill ordinary fuel; drafts begin above standing height so grounded jumps remain available. |
| Moonlit Causeway | Opposing currents and fifty-one moving rafts between fixed islands. Sections alternate rising rafts and sideways drift, then strengthen the undertow. | Reach the upper valves, then time takeoff and landing against the moving tide platforms. | Lower catch ledges make missed raft landings recoverable; rescue relays secure each section. |
| Ember Oath | Brittle ledges, pulsing furnace vents and a lower maintenance walk. | Commit the climb before a ledge falls, cross the vents during safe phases, and regain height after a missed crossing. | Brittle ledges restore. The maintenance walk provides a fuel-restoring recovery route instead of a guaranteed fall death. |

The original final checkpoint remains before the crossing foyer. Forty-two appended rescue relays preserve every old checkpoint index and ensure that a failed crossing can be retried without replaying earlier bosses. Relays advance within a realm and can become active again when the player switches realms, so choosing water or fire before wind does not invalidate wind's recovery points. Completing an oath disables its entry portal so it cannot repeatedly capture a returning player. Each trial has a visible retreat portal at its first landing, including after loading a saved checkpoint. Portal entrances have a gap wider than the player's body so the arrival lock can release between them. Portals clear velocity and debounce arrivals; an arrival is not a momentum exploit.

## Art and animation contract

Source illustrations establish anatomy and material before physics registration. Messenger, bird and bridge motifs in the opening are symbolic characters in original fiction; this revision does not claim that they are newly rigged playable Hanuman or Jatayu characters. Gameplay uses the courier, existing companion controllers and rebuilt enemy families. A new playable mythical character requires its own approved motion strip, stable foot pivot, attack sockets, hurt bounds, anticipation, contact and recovery before admission.

Wind particles must show direction, water must imply current, and furnace vents must expose a readable safe interval. Shutters and lever effects belong to machinery rather than blue circular enemy halos. Cannon shots must originate at the actual barrels. A full-body dragon animation must coordinate neck, torso, wings, tail and recoil; translating an idle image is not sufficient.

## Duration and evidence

The design target is **ten minutes or more per level**, combining the original rooms, three boss fights, nine crossing puzzles, exploration and conversations. The three crossings target three to four minutes each for a learning player; this is a design estimate, not a human timing measurement. Skilled players are allowed to finish faster. Automated traversal duration, deaths and gate completion are reported separately in the legal campaign artifacts. Human/device timing and fastest completion remain separate acceptance checks.

The final nine legal campaigns completed in **652.37–702.96 seconds** on 17 October 2026. Every run opened all twenty-one seals, entered and returned from all three crossings, defeated their guardians after live attacks, and witnessed the final guardian transform and attack as a dragon. Eight runs finished without deaths; Extinction used one legitimate checkpoint retry. The automated route advances dialogue immediately and knows its destinations. These are measured gameplay durations, not human timing or Android performance measurements. See [the source-verified campaign summary](../artifacts/epic-rebuild/campaign-summary.json).

The driver recovers from missed moving rafts with ordinary vertical double jumps. For the highest furnace recovery, it stands on the maintenance walk until fuel is sufficient, then jumps and uses finite thrust. This is normal resource recovery, not an artificial duration gate. Isolated zero-fuel recovery fixtures pass in all five worlds; the complete campaigns independently reach and finish those routes through ordinary movement.

`tests/crossings.test.js` is an isolated data, interaction and persistence fixture; it deliberately places the player beside switches. `scripts/combat-route.mjs` uses movement, combat, interaction and portal contact, records its inputs and measures the actual campaign. Neither is physical-device comfort or performance evidence.
