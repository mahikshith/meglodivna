# Ramayana inspiration and the Lantern journey

The Lantern story follows a courier who becomes responsible for a captive community. Its rescue mission, difficult crossings, animal allies and victory through cooperation draw on the Ramayana. Aether, Mira, the Regent, the three rune stones and the cannon dragon are original fiction. They are not representations of Rama, Sita, Ravana or a canonical battle. The illustrated sanctuary palette, natural stone, ivory armor and amber light remain the visual foundation.

## Characters and source traditions

These notes use specific episodes from the Sanskrit Valmiki Ramayana in the linked translations. Other Ramayana traditions differ. A narrative obstacle is not automatically a permanent biological weakness; fuel meters, attack cooldowns, damage windows and equipment progression below are game inventions.

## The playable story

The opening has three six-second beats: the Regent binds Orren and the sanctuary; Aether promises to bring Mira's brother home; Mira names the three stones and the ordered Wind, Water and Fire route. It lasts eighteen seconds when allowed to finish, with skip handled by the interface. Its art depicts the promise, the crossing and the confrontation.

The journey progresses from **last courier**, to **bearer of embers**, to **wayfinder of the lost**, to **knight of the lantern**. These titles follow the collected stones; they do not silently alter weapon statistics. Mira supplies the personal motive. Orren reveals the engine's failure and the guardian's recovery window. The three restored crossings make the community's rescue an action the player performs before the final confrontation. Each chapter has an entry and return conversation, a persistent HUD objective and a guardian goal. The ending recounts Orren's rescue and Aether's return as lantern keeper. The mythic rescue, messenger's vow and built crossing are motifs from [Sundara Kanda 1](https://valmikiramayan.net/utf8/sundara/sarga1/sundararoman1.htm), [Ayodhya Kanda 112](https://ramayana.info/story/ayodhya/112/) and [Yuddha Kanda 22](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/yuddha/sarga22/yuddhasans22.htm). Aether, Mira and Orren remain original fictional characters.

The Water chapter leaves a safe causeway for families following Aether; that shared refuge is an original fiction echo of the [collective shelter in Bhagavata Purana 10.25](https://bhagavata.org/canto10/chapter25.html). The Fire chapter rejects leaving any captive behind, and the final heart gate requires all three crossings to be restored. This uses fairness as a theme from the [Mahabharata's Yaksha episode, Vana Parva 311](https://www.ibiblio.org/sripedia/ebooks/mb/m03/m03311.htm), where Yudhishthira chooses Nakula. Neither episode is recreated as a literal game scene or assigned an invented weakness.

The testing armory may expose every power immediately. That mode must be visibly identified as a test configuration; journey titles still communicate narrative progress independently of unlocked equipment.

## Portal crossings and action puzzles

The main route now leads to **one lit gate** in the heart foyer. It enters Wind, then Water, then Fire, opening the next chapter only after the current guardian dies. Return arrivals land west of the shared gate so moving forward continues the story. Every world keeps its original main route and three crossing trials. Each starts with three elevated, guarded teaching courts, then continues through 28 hand-authored landings in four named sections. Five safe relays per crossing divide the questions into recoverable beats. Landing at each high lantern opens its shutter automatically; the guardian's defeat opens its heart seal. The route uses no forced timer or locked waiting room.

| Crossing | Physical identity | Player action | Recovery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Windward Canopy | Upward and lateral airflow around high stone bells, then four climbs with alternating crosswinds and elevations up to 265 units. | Catch a draft and land at the teaching lantern; in each section, combine a changing gust with a high bell to open the next shutter. | Broad relay stones refill ordinary fuel; drafts begin above standing height so grounded jumps remain available. |
| Moonlit Causeway | Opposing currents and fourteen required moving rafts, including three teaching rafts. Sections alternate rising rafts and sideways drift. | Board a moving raft, land at the teaching lantern, then combine timed takeoff, changing current and high mooring stones to open four more shutters. | Lower catch ledges make missed raft landings recoverable; safe relays secure each section. |
| Ember Oath | Seven brittle course ledges, seven timed vents and a lower maintenance walk. | Jump from a brittle step to the teaching lantern, then pair each vent's quiet interval with a high seal landing under combat pressure. | Brittle ledges restore. The maintenance walk allows fuel to recharge before the high recovery climb. |

The original final checkpoint remains before the crossing foyer. Fifteen appended rescue relays allow a failed crossing to resume without replaying earlier bosses. v0.9 saves used fourteen relays per realm; layout-version migration maps each old relay proportionally into the shorter corresponding chapter while preserving gear, claims and encountered gates. Completing a chapter disables its entrance and activates the next one in that same visible location. Each trial has a retreat portal at its first landing, including after loading a saved checkpoint. Return arrivals land more than a body width west of the gate; contact locks release before moving into the next entrance. Portals clear velocity and debounce arrivals. The path lantern changes from cool to amber when its shutter opens, the gate vanishes, and the HUD names the next action. These puzzles follow the level-design pattern of introducing one reliable action, practising it, then combining it with hazards and combat, as described in Matt Thorson's [Celeste workshop](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024307/Level-Design-Workshop-Designing-Celeste) and the [Portal 2 puzzle exercise](https://media.steampowered.com/apps/twp/pdf/The%20Portal%20Rube%20Goldberg.pdf).

## Art and animation contract

Source illustrations establish anatomy and material before physics registration. Messenger, bird and bridge motifs in the opening are symbolic characters in original fiction; this revision does not claim that they are newly rigged playable Hanuman or Jatayu characters. Gameplay uses the courier, existing companion controllers and rebuilt enemy families. A new playable mythical character requires its own approved motion strip, stable foot pivot, attack sockets, hurt bounds, anticipation, contact and recovery before admission.

Wind particles must show direction, water must imply current, and furnace vents must expose a readable safe interval. Path lanterns and shutters belong to scenery rather than blue circular enemy halos. Cannon shots must originate at the actual barrels. A full-body dragon animation must coordinate neck, torso, wings, tail and recoil; translating an idle image is not sufficient.

## Duration and evidence

The design target is **ten minutes or more per level** for a first human playthrough, combining the original rooms, three boss fights, the crossing puzzles, exploration and conversations. This remains unverified without a human playtest. The legal automated driver knows the route and advances dialogue immediately. The revised Hollow Sanctuary Forge route takes **313.56 simulated seconds** with zero deaths; Wind, Water and Fire take **65.77, 72.31 and 72.49 seconds** of that run. It opens all 33 gates, claims eight story scenes and sees the final dragon transform and attack. All nine corrected campaign variants win in **293.76–361.05 simulated seconds**, with three legitimate retries across the matrix. This is legal simulation evidence, not human timing or Android performance evidence. See [the correction campaign summary](../artifacts/playtest-correction/campaign-summary.json) and [the Forge route report](../artifacts/playtest-correction/combat-route-report.json).

The archived v0.9 nine legal campaigns completed in **652.37–702.96 seconds** on 17 October 2026. Every run opened all twenty-one seals, entered and returned from all three crossings, defeated their guardians after live attacks, and witnessed the final guardian transform and attack as a dragon. Eight runs finished without deaths; Extinction used one legitimate checkpoint retry. The automated route advances dialogue immediately and knows its destinations. These are measured gameplay durations, not human timing or Android performance measurements. This is historical evidence for the earlier portal layout. See [the archived campaign summary](../artifacts/epic-rebuild/campaign-summary.json).

`tests/crossings.test.js` is an isolated data, landing and persistence fixture; it deliberately places the player on lanterns. `scripts/combat-route.mjs --correction` uses movement, combat, dialogue and portal contact, records its inputs under `artifacts/playtest-correction/` and measures the revised campaign. Neither is physical-device comfort or performance evidence.
